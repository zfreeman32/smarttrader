What's Missing or Underweight
Here are the areas where I see gaps compared to what the research literature and successful quant practitioners emphasize:
1. No explicit regime detection or conditional model gating. This is probably the most critical gap. Recent literature stresses the non-stationary nature and presence of drastic structural changes in the financial domain, finding that conventional ML approaches are often unable or slow to adapt to changes in the price-generation process. ResearchGate Your pipeline trains one model that's expected to work across all market conditions. Research on meta-labeling — a technique that decouples directional prediction from position sizing, enabling dynamic trade suppression in adverse market conditions Wikipedia — suggests that a secondary model evaluating when your primary signal is reliable can dramatically improve risk-adjusted returns. You have the infrastructure for this (quality scores, confluence counts), but no explicit regime-aware gating layer.
3. Consider upgrading from walk-forward to Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation (CPCV). You currently use expanding-window purged WF, which is good but not optimal. Walk-forward backtesting preserves temporal order but evaluates the model on a single sequence of test sets, leading to high variance in performance estimation contingent on a specific historical path. CPCV addresses this by systematically constructing multiple train-test splits, producing a distribution of out-of-sample performance estimates. Wikipedia CPCV would give you a distribution of performance metrics rather than a single path, letting you compute the Probability of Backtest Overfitting — a much stronger statistical guarantee.
4. No SHAP-based feature interpretability. You compute random-forest importance and mutual information, but the literature strongly favors SHAP values for tree-based models because they provide per-prediction explanations and handle feature interactions more faithfully than aggregate importance measures. For a trading system, understanding why the model fired on a specific bar is critical for trust, debugging, and avoiding spurious signals. This is especially important given your large feature space (hundreds of features after transforms and lags).
5. No meta-labeling layer. Meta-labeling has become one of the most widely adopted techniques in professional quantitative finance because it dramatically enhances the reliability of trading signals and improves risk-adjusted returns. Substack Your pipeline treats side detection and confidence estimation as one task. Separating them — letting the OTE detector generate candidates, then training a secondary classifier to predict which candidates will actually succeed — is a natural extension of your existing architecture. Your quality scores and confluence features are already the kind of inputs a meta-model would consume.
6. No alternative bar types. López de Prado argues strongly against time bars (which is what your 5-minute OHLCV is) because markets don't process information at constant rates. Volume bars or dollar bars have better statistical properties (less serial correlation, closer to normality). This is a structural limitation worth at least investigating.
7. No synthetic data testing or adversarial stress testing. Your pipeline validates on historical data only. Research suggests that testing on synthetic data (generated via models like Heston stochastic volatility or regime-switching processes) can reveal fragilities that historical data alone won't expose. If a researcher tests many different strategy ideas on the same dataset using rigorous CPCV, they will eventually find something that looks robust by pure chance — CPCV cannot protect against this meta-overfitting. Quantbeckman
9. Synthetic label features. An approach from semi-supervised learning research: train a simple, fast model (like a small random forest) on your labels with a handful of obvious features, then use that model's predicted probabilities as a feature for the main model. This sounds circular but works because the simple model captures the low-hanging-fruit patterns, and its prediction residuals (where it's uncertain or wrong) highlight the hard cases where the deep model needs to focus. The simple model's confidence can also be used as a feature weighting signal — high confidence from the simple model on a negative sample makes it a more informative negative example.

Post-Training:
1. No concept drift monitoring or automated retraining triggers. Research examining 32 datasets across four industries found that 91% of ML models experience degradation over time, with models left unchanged for six months or longer seeing error rates jump 35% on new data. SmartDev Your pipeline trains once and exports artifacts, but has no mechanism to detect when the model's probability distributions have shifted or when feature importance has meaningfully changed. In FX markets specifically, regime shifts (central bank policy changes, volatility regime transitions) can invalidate learned patterns rapidly.
8. Transaction costs and execution modeling are absent. The pipeline stops at signal detection. Without realistic slippage, spread, and fill assumptions baked into the evaluation, you can't know if detectable signals survive execution friction — especially on 5-minute EUR/USD where spreads and microstructure matter.
